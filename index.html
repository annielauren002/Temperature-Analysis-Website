January Temperature Trends for Select Years (1980-2025)

A Data Science Project by Annie Francis | Data Scientist | Environmental Enthusiast

Project Overview

This project explores January temperature trends in Annapolis, Maryland across six key years between 1980 and 2025. Using a series of interactive visualizations—including line charts, box plots, heatmaps, and bar graphs—this analysis highlights the variability and volatility of winter temperatures in a coastal Mid-Atlantic city. Rather than revealing a simple warming trend, the data exposes erratic fluctuations shaped by regional climate dynamics like the North Atlantic Oscillation, polar vortex shifts, and oceanic influences from the nearby Chesapeake Bay.

By comparing both daily and monthly temperature patterns, this dashboard provides a nuanced view of how climate instability, not just long-term warming, impacts local conditions. These insights are essential for climate preparedness, infrastructure resilience, and ecological awareness in vulnerable coastal communities.

Average Temperature Comparison

The analysis of January temperatures in Annapolis, Maryland over a 45-year period reveals fascinating patterns in our changing climate. The data shows dramatic fluctuations rather than a simple linear trend, challenging common assumptions about climate change in the Chesapeake Bay region. This analysis underscores the importance of long-term data collection and the complex nature of climate patterns in coastal regions like the Chesapeake Bay. While global warming remains a clear trend worldwide, regional and seasonal variations create a more nuanced picture that demands continued monitoring and research.

Pattern Analysis:

  • No consistent warming or cooling trend is visible across the four-and-a-half decades
  • Major temperature peaks occurred in 1990 and 2020 (approximately 41°F)
  • Significant temperature drops followed each peak, with the post-2020 decline being especially dramatic
  • The lowest recorded average (31.2°F) occurred in 2025, representing a sharp 10-degree drop from just five years earlier
  • Overall temperature range spans approximately 12°F (31-42°F)

Regional Context:

  • These fluctuations reflect the complex climate dynamics of the Mid-Atlantic region
  • The Chesapeake Bay typically moderates temperature extremes, making these sharp variations particularly noteworthy
  • The patterns align with known climate oscillations affecting the Eastern Seaboard, including the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) and El Niño events
  • Recent polar vortex disruptions likely contributed to the sharp post-2020 temperature decline

Environmental Implications:

  • Annapolis faces unique climate challenges as a coastal community experiencing both warming waters and variable winter conditions
  • The accelerated warming of the Chesapeake Bay (warming faster than global averages) creates a complex interplay with atmospheric patterns
  • Urban development and sea level rise in the region further complicate the temperature dynamics
  • These patterns highlight the importance of distinguishing between global trends and local climate variations

Daily Temperature Comparison

The analysis of average daily temperatures for January across six key years (1980, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020, and 2025) reveals just how variable winter weather can be in Annapolis, Maryland. Instead of a smooth progression over time, the graph highlights chaotic daily swings in temperature, often unrelated to broader long-term patterns. This granular view illustrates how regional climate systems, atmospheric disruptions, and even individual storm systems can strongly impact short-term climate behavior. It’s a reminder that while global trends are crucial, local daily patterns offer an essential window into real-life climate impacts.

Pattern Analysis:

  • No year displayed a stable, predictable progression of temperatures across January—daily values frequently rose or dropped by 10–20°F overnight
  • 1990 and 2020 featured warm spikes early in the month, exceeding 55–60°F, while 2000 dropped below 20°F multiple times, especially in the latter half
  • 2025, though the coldest in overall monthly averages, still showed brief warm spells—a sign of volatility more than consistent cold
  • Every year showed sharp dips mid-month, possibly tied to mid-winter storm activity or polar vortex effects
  • Range of daily temperatures within a single year often spanned over 30°F, demonstrating extreme variability even within a short timeframe

Regional Context

  • Daily fluctuations reflect the highly dynamic winter weather patterns of the Mid-Atlantic U.S.
  • Annapolis is especially vulnerable to sharp temperature changes due to its location between the Atlantic Ocean and continental weather systems, including cold Arctic air masses and warm oceanic surges
  • These sharp contrasts are often influenced by regional patterns like the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) and Arctic Oscillation, which impact jet stream positioning and cold air outbreaks

Environmental Implications

  • Volatile winter temperatures can wreak havoc on ecosystems, agriculture, and infrastructure—especially in coastal cities like Annapolis
  • Rapid freezing and thawing cycles threaten local flora, damage roads and infrastructure, and disrupt wildlife behavior
  • Local residents may feel climate change more in these daily extremes than in slow average warming
  • The data underscores the importance of daily monitoring systems and emergency planning, as extreme cold or warm events can occur suddenly and unpredictably

Temperature Variability By Year

This box plot offers a deeper dive into the distribution and variability of January temperatures across six selected years, from 1980 to 2025. Rather than looking at daily or yearly averages alone, this visualization highlights the spread, central tendency, and extreme outliers for each year. The red dashed line at 32°F serves as a key reference point for freezing temperatures, illustrating how often and how drastically daily temperatures dip below freezing—an important factor for ecosystems, infrastructure, and energy demand.

Pattern Analysis:

  • 2000 shows the widest spread, with temperatures ranging from below 20°F to nearly 60°F—marking it as a year of extreme winter volatility
  • 2020 and 1990 exhibit the highest median January temperatures (above 40°F), reinforcing prior analyses showing warming spikes in those years
  • 2025 and 2000 show frequent daily dips below freezing, with 2025 having a notably low median (~31°F), dipping just under the freezing line
  • 2010 and 1980 reflect more moderate spreads compared to 2000 or 2020, but still show meaningful variability
  • Outliers appear in every year, with extreme highs above 55°F and lows near 15°F, emphasizing how unpredictable January weather can be

Regional Context

  • Annapolis winters are shaped by competing oceanic warmth from the Chesapeake Bay and Arctic intrusions, which explains the wide interquartile ranges
  • El Niño/La Niña cycles, the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO), and polar vortex events likely explain years like 2000 and 2025 where extreme variability or cold dominates
  • Despite an upward global trend in temperatures, local effects in Annapolis—such as bay breezes, urban development, and regional storm tracks—shape these inconsistent winter patterns

Environmental Implications

  • Years like 2025 pose major challenges for public services and wildlife, as consistently freezing conditions increase heating demands and freeze–thaw damage risks
  • Years like 2000, with high variability, may confuse plant life, causing premature blooming or crop damage from cold snaps
  • Infrastructure planning must account for years with large temperature swings and numerous freeze events, even if average temps suggest milder conditions
  • The plot emphasizes the need for localized climate adaptation strategies, especially in vulnerable coastal cities like Annapolis

Decade-by-Decade Temperature Comparison

This bar chart presents a clear, comparative view of average January temperatures in Annapolis, MD, over six selected years from 1980 to 2025. Unlike line graphs or box plots that show fluctuations and variability, this visualization offers a concise snapshot of how each year stacks up in terms of overall warmth. The use of a color gradient reinforces key takeaways — with 2025 shown in deep red as the coldest, and 2020/1990 in dark blue as the warmest years in the dataset.

Pattern Analysis:

  • Warmest Years: 2020 (40.6°F) and 1990 (40.3°F) were the two hottest Januarys, both breaking the 40°F mark
  • Coldest Year: 2025 recorded an average of 31.2°F, a nearly 10-degree drop from 2020
  • Gradual Trends?: No steady warming or cooling trend is visible — temperatures rise and fall irregularly across the decades
  • Most Consistent Years: 2000 and 2010 hovered close together (around 34–35°F), suggesting more average winter conditions
  • The total range spans just under 10°F, but that difference can mean the contrast between frequent freezes and mostly above-freezing days

Regional Context

  • This snapshot aligns with episodic warming and cooling patterns common in the Mid-Atlantic, driven by shifting air masses and large-scale climate oscillations
  • The sharp contrast between 2020 and 2025 highlights the impact of Arctic air events, likely tied to polar vortex shifts or NAO phase changes
  • The Chesapeake Bay's influence may have buffered some years (like 1990 and 2020) from deeper freezes, while others (2025) reflect broader regional cold outbreaks

Environmental Implications

  • A shift from 40.6°F in 2020 to 31.2°F in 2025 affects everything from wildlife hibernation cycles to utility demand
  • Colder years increase energy costs, road maintenance needs, and stress on coastal infrastructure due to freeze–thaw cycles
  • These inconsistencies make it difficult to rely solely on annual averages — reinforcing the importance of looking at local, seasonal, and daily data alongside global trends
  • The visualization supports the idea that climate instability, rather than linear warming, may be the more pressing local concern in the short term

Temperature Heatmap Analysis

This heatmap visualization reveals detailed day-by-day temperature patterns for January across six selected years (1980–2025) in Annapolis, Maryland. The chart uses a red-to-blue color scale to show warm and cold days, respectively, providing an intuitive visual cue to spot unusual heat or cold events. By comparing each row, you can see how winter temperatures have fluctuated across decades—not just in averages, but in how temperatures shift throughout the month.

Pattern Analysis:

  • 2000 stands out for extreme contrast — early January has multiple hot spikes (55–60°F) while the second half of the month plunges into cold blues, showing rapid swings
  • 020 has a more balanced but fluctuating pattern — early warmth, a mid-month cold snap, and then milder temps again by the end
  • 2010 appears the most stable, with much of the month hovering in the mid-blue range (30s)
  • 1990 and 2000 both show intense heat events clustered around mid-month
  • 2025, though not shown explicitly here, would likely display deep blue coloring if included—suggesting sustained cold similar to 2010

Regional Context

  • The Mid-Atlantic is a hotspot for temperature variability due to clashing air masses—warm southern flow vs. Arctic intrusions
  • This pattern often leads to freeze–thaw cycles within a single week, clearly visible in years like 2000 and 1990
  • The Chesapeake Bay usually moderates extreme temps, but this heatmap shows several exceptions, likely due to NAO phases, polar vortex shifts, and coastal storm tracks

Environmental Implications

  • This kind of detailed data is vital for agriculture, energy planning, and emergency response, as day-to-day variations can cause real disruption
  • High variability increases the risk of crop damage, infrastructure wear, and health impacts from sudden freezes or warm-ups
  • The recurring pattern of abrupt warm and cold spells shows why yearly averages aren’t enough—local systems must prepare for increasingly chaotic winter conditions
  • Encourages investment in real-time monitoring and climate adaptation tools for vulnerable coastal regions like Annapolis

Small Multiples Comparison

This multi-panel plot compares daily January temperatures across six key years: 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010, 2020, and 2025. By isolating each year in its own mini-chart, the visualization makes it easier to observe unique temperature trends and disruptions within each month—without the confusion of overlapping lines. This format highlights how wildly different January can feel from year to year, especially in a transitional coastal region like Annapolis.

Pattern Analysis:

  • 1990 and 2020 stand out for consistently high temperatures, with several days peaking above 50°F
  • 2000 shows a steep decline from early-month warmth (near 60°F) to prolonged cold at the end—one of the most dramatic intra-month shifts
  • 2025 remains relatively cold throughout, dipping below freezing for much of the month before a brief rebound at the end
  • 2010 shows a sudden spike near day 27, likely tied to a short-lived warm front or coastal storm anomaly
  • 1980 was moderate, fluctuating between 30°F and 45°F but never reaching the extremes of later years

Regional Context

  • This visualization reinforces how January weather in the Mid-Atlantic is governed by chaotic air mass interactions, with warm southern systems and Arctic air battling for control
  • 2020 and 1990 may reflect positive NAO phases, favoring warmer, wetter conditions along the coast
  • 2000 and 2025 could indicate negative NAO or polar vortex breakdowns, allowing cold Arctic air to dominate

Environmental Implications

  • The lack of consistency from year to year complicates climate planning: what looks like a mild winter one year could turn dangerously cold the next
  • Rapid warm/cold swings within the same month, like in 2000, are particularly damaging for agriculture, roadways, and ecosystems
  • The 2025 pattern of sustained cold can increase energy demand, heating costs, and health risks, particularly in vulnerable populations
  • This reinforces the need for resilient infrastructure and flexible adaptation strategies, especially in coastal cities sensitive to both air and water temperature changes

Key Findings and Implications

The analysis reveals that January temperatures in Annapolis, Maryland are marked more by volatility and irregularity than a straightforward warming trend. While 1990 and 2020 recorded notably high average temperatures, 2025 showed a dramatic cold drop, underscoring the region’s susceptibility to polar air intrusions and coastal weather dynamics. Daily fluctuations of 20–30°F within a single month were common, presenting real challenges for agriculture, infrastructure, and energy systems. These findings highlight the importance of localized climate monitoring, and they reinforce the need to plan for instability rather than just gradual warming in coastal Mid-Atlantic communities.
